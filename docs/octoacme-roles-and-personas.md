# OctoAcme Personas

This document defines typical roles and responsibilities used in OctoAcme project docs and exercises.

---

## Developers

### Role Summary
Developers design, build, test, and deliver software components. They collaborate with product and project leads to implement features that meet acceptance criteria and quality standards.

### Responsibilities
- Implement features and fixes to meet acceptance criteria
- Write and maintain tests and documentation
- Participate in design and code reviews
- Assist in estimating and planning work
- Help identify technical risks and propose mitigations

### Goals
- Deliver reliable, maintainable code
- Reduce cycle time from idea to production
- Maintain high test coverage and observability

### Typical Communication
- Daily standups and sprint planning
- PR descriptions and code review comments
- Technical design docs when needed

---

## Product Managers

### Role Summary
Product Managers define what should be built to deliver customer and business value. They own the product vision, prioritize the backlog, and measure outcomes.

### Responsibilities
- Define problem statements and success metrics
- Prioritize the roadmap and backlog
- Collaborate with stakeholders and engineering on trade-offs
- Validate solutions through user research and metrics

### Goals
- Maximize customer value and impact
- Make clear, data-driven prioritization decisions
- Ensure product-market fit and usability

### Typical Communication
- Weekly alignment with PM and engineering leads
- Roadmap updates and stakeholder briefings
- Acceptance criteria and feature specs

---

## Project Managers

### Role Summary
Project Managers coordinate delivery activities, manage schedules, risks, and communications. They enable the team to deliver on commitments efficiently.

### Responsibilities
- Create and maintain project plans and timelines
- Manage risks, dependencies, and resource constraints
- Facilitate meetings (kickoff, planning, retrospectives)
- Ensure consistent project documentation and status reporting
- Coordinate cross-team and stakeholder communication

### Goals
- Deliver projects on time and within scope
- Minimize unplanned work and escalations
- Maintain transparency and alignment across stakeholders

### Typical Communication
- Weekly status updates and stakeholder reports
- Risk registers and decision logs
- Coordination via project boards and meeting facilitation

---

## Business Sponsor / Executive Sponsor

### Role Summary
Business Sponsors provide strategic sponsorship, funding support, and executive-level decision backing. They ensure project outcomes stay aligned to business priorities and accountable value delivery.

### Responsibilities
- Confirm business mandate, priorities, and success criteria at initiation
- Approve major scope, funding, or timeline trade-off decisions
- Support escalation resolution when cross-team blockers persist
- Reinforce accountability for outcomes across delivery leaders

### Goals
- Maximize business impact and strategic alignment
- Keep decision gates timely and explicit
- Reduce delivery risk caused by unclear executive direction

### Decision Rights
- Go/no-go sponsorship and continuation decisions
- Approval of major scope or funding trade-offs
- Final escalation support when delivery issues exceed team authority

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Validate business case, sponsorship, and success outcomes
- **Planning:** Confirm milestones and key decision gates
- **Execution & Tracking:** Review health, risks, and escalations at checkpoints
- **Release & Deployment:** Confirm readiness from a business-impact perspective
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Validate outcomes versus mandate and lessons to scale

### Typical Communication
- Sponsor briefings and milestone reviews with Project Managers and Product Managers
- Escalation meetings with Delivery and Technical leads
- Executive summaries focused on outcomes, risks, and decisions needed

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Partners with **Product Managers** on value trade-offs and strategic priorities
- Partners with **Project Managers** on risk visibility and decision timing
- Engages **Stakeholders** for alignment and executive communications
- Supports **Delivery Leads/Engineering Managers** when organizational constraints block progress

---

## Delivery Lead / Engineering Manager

### Role Summary
Delivery Leads (or Engineering Managers) own engineering delivery health, team capacity, and execution reliability across the project lifecycle.

### Responsibilities
- Plan and balance engineering capacity with roadmap commitments
- Remove delivery blockers and manage technical execution risks
- Align Developers, QA/Testing, and Technical Leads on delivery scope and sequencing
- Track delivery health indicators and raise early escalations

### Goals
- Predictable delivery and sustainable team throughput
- Clear ownership and healthy engineering collaboration
- Reduced risk from hidden dependencies or staffing gaps

### Decision Rights
- Engineering staffing and prioritization recommendations
- Sequencing and execution adjustments within approved scope
- Escalation of delivery risks requiring sponsor or PM decisions

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Assess feasibility and capacity assumptions
- **Planning:** Co-create implementation plans, estimates, and dependency sequencing
- **Execution & Tracking:** Monitor flow, unblock teams, and tune delivery cadence
- **Release & Deployment:** Confirm engineering readiness and on-call/ownership coverage
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Lead engineering improvement actions

### Typical Communication
- Daily or frequent syncs with Developers and QA/Testing
- Weekly planning and risk sync with Project Managers and Product Managers
- Delivery health updates for sponsors and stakeholders as needed

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Works with **Project Managers** on timelines, dependencies, and risk responses
- Collaborates with **Product Managers** on scope trade-offs and sequencing
- Partners with **Developers** and **QA/Testing** for execution quality and predictability
- Coordinates with **Technical Leads/Architects** on design risk and implementation fit

---

## Technical Lead / Architect

### Role Summary
Technical Leads (or Architects) guide solution architecture, integration strategy, and technical risk management to keep delivery maintainable and scalable.

### Responsibilities
- Define and communicate architecture direction and constraints
- Identify and mitigate technical risks and integration impacts
- Guide design reviews, implementation patterns, and non-functional quality
- Partner on security, observability, and operational readiness requirements

### Goals
- Build maintainable systems that meet functional and non-functional requirements
- Reduce rework from unclear architecture decisions
- Keep technical debt and platform risk visible and manageable

### Decision Rights
- Technical design standards and architecture recommendations
- Approval or rejection of implementation approaches against architecture constraints
- Escalation of technical risks that affect scope, timeline, or reliability

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Validate feasibility and architectural impact
- **Planning:** Define technical approach, dependencies, and risk controls
- **Execution & Tracking:** Guide technical decisions and resolve integration blockers
- **Release & Deployment:** Ensure production readiness for reliability and operability
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Review technical outcomes and debt follow-up

### Typical Communication
- Design docs and architecture review discussions
- PR and technical review guidance for Developers
- Risk and trade-off briefings with Product and Project Managers

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Directly supports **Developers** with implementation guidance
- Coordinates with **Security/Privacy/Compliance** on control requirements
- Works with **Release/Operations/SRE** on deployment and reliability design
- Partners with **Product Managers** to balance feasibility, quality, and customer value

## UX/UI or Service Designer

### Role Summary
UX/UI or Service Designers represent user needs in solution design, ensuring workflows are usable, accessible, and aligned to customer outcomes.

### Responsibilities
- Define user journeys, interaction flows, and design constraints
- Validate usability through prototypes, feedback, or lightweight research
- Translate user needs into acceptance considerations with Product and QA
- Promote accessibility and consistency across experiences

### Goals
- Improve customer experience and usability outcomes
- Reduce defects caused by unclear requirements or inconsistent workflows
- Increase confidence that delivered features solve user problems

### Decision Rights
- Recommendations on design patterns and usability priorities
- Approval input on UX acceptance criteria before delivery sign-off
- Escalation when requirements create known usability or accessibility risks

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Frame user problems and target experience outcomes
- **Planning:** Define workflows and acceptance expectations
- **Execution & Tracking:** Refine designs based on implementation feedback
- **Release & Deployment:** Support readiness for customer-facing changes and messaging
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Provide user-impact insights for improvement actions

### Typical Communication
- Design reviews with Developers and Product Managers
- Acceptance criteria clarification with QA/Testing and Project Managers
- Demo and feedback sessions with stakeholders and customer-facing teams

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Partners with **Product Managers** on problem framing and validation
- Works with **Developers** to translate design intent into implementation detail
- Collaborates with **QA/Testing** on user workflow validation
- Syncs with **Customer Success/Support** for real-world usability feedback

---

## Security, Privacy, or Compliance Representative

### Role Summary
Security, Privacy, or Compliance representatives ensure regulatory, policy, and control requirements are addressed throughout planning, delivery, and release.

### Responsibilities
- Identify applicable security, privacy, and compliance obligations
- Review threat, control, and data handling risks with delivery teams
- Advise on required approvals, evidence, and audit readiness
- Support incident readiness and post-incident corrective actions

### Goals
- Prevent avoidable security/privacy risks and compliance gaps
- Ensure release decisions include control readiness
- Reduce late-stage rework caused by missing governance requirements

### Decision Rights
- Required control recommendations and compliance gating input
- Escalation authority for unresolved high-risk findings
- Approval input for release readiness where policy requires

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Highlight obligations and risk profile
- **Planning:** Define controls, checkpoints, and required evidence
- **Execution & Tracking:** Review implementation risk and remediation progress
- **Release & Deployment:** Validate control readiness and residual risk decisions
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Capture governance lessons and follow-up actions

### Typical Communication
- Risk reviews with Technical Leads, Developers, and QA/Testing
- Compliance checkpoint updates with Project Managers
- Escalation summaries for sponsors and stakeholders when needed

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Partners with **Technical Leads/Architects** on secure design and controls
- Supports **Developers** and **QA/Testing** with remediation priorities and validation
- Coordinates with **Release/Operations/SRE** on production control verification
- Keeps **Project Managers** informed of gating or evidence requirements

## Data/Analytics or Observability Lead

### Role Summary
Data/Analytics or Observability Leads ensure teams can measure product outcomes and system health through clear metrics, instrumentation, and reporting.

### Responsibilities
- Define outcome metrics and monitoring requirements
- Guide instrumentation, event definitions, dashboards, and alerts
- Validate data quality and reliability of reporting signals
- Provide analysis that informs prioritization and continuous improvement

### Goals
- Ensure decisions are supported by reliable evidence
- Detect product or operational issues early
- Improve traceability from delivery output to business/customer impact

### Decision Rights
- Measurement design and observability standard recommendations
- Alerting and dashboard requirement recommendations
- Escalation when critical metrics or telemetry are missing or unreliable

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Shape measurable success criteria with Product and Sponsor roles
- **Planning:** Define instrumentation and reporting work in delivery scope
- **Execution & Tracking:** Validate telemetry implementation and reporting quality
- **Release & Deployment:** Confirm monitoring and alert readiness for go-live
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Analyze outcomes and feed improvements into backlog

### Typical Communication
- Metric definition workshops with Product Managers
- Instrumentation planning with Developers and Technical Leads
- Health and outcome reports to Project Managers and Stakeholders

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Partners with **Product Managers** on north-star and feature-level success metrics
- Works with **Developers** and **Technical Leads** on instrumentation implementation
- Coordinates with **Release/Operations/SRE** on monitoring coverage and alerts
- Informs **Stakeholders** with outcome-focused reporting

## Release/Operations/SRE Lead

### Role Summary
Release/Operations/SRE Leads own deployment readiness, service reliability, and production operations practices needed for safe, repeatable releases.

### Responsibilities
- Define release readiness, runbooks, rollback, and incident response expectations
- Validate operational controls, monitoring, and support handover readiness
- Coordinate release windows and production change communication
- Lead operational response during release and early-life support

### Goals
- Deliver reliable releases with minimal disruption
- Shorten recovery time when incidents occur
- Improve operational maturity through repeatable release practices

### Decision Rights
- Operational readiness recommendations and release risk input
- Pause/escalate recommendation when release controls are insufficient
- Incident coordination authority during deployment events

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Identify operational constraints and reliability expectations
- **Planning:** Define release strategy, environments, and readiness criteria
- **Execution & Tracking:** Validate runbook, monitoring, and reliability preparation
- **Release & Deployment:** Coordinate execution, verification, and rollback decisions
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Review incidents and operational lessons learned

### Typical Communication
- Release readiness checklists with Developers, QA/Testing, and Security representatives
- Go/no-go syncs with Project Managers and Delivery Leads
- Incident and post-release reports to stakeholders and customer-facing teams

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Collaborates with **Developers** and **QA/Testing** on deployment and verification readiness
- Partners with **Security/Privacy/Compliance** on production control validation
- Aligns with **Project Managers** on release timelines and escalation flow
- Coordinates with **Customer Success/Support** for release communications and incident handling

### Responsibilities
- Provide customer feedback, recurring issue trends, and support constraints
- Prepare support playbooks, communication templates, and readiness updates
- Validate that release plans include customer-facing operational needs
- Feed post-release insights into backlog prioritization

### Goals
- Improve adoption and customer outcomes after release
- Reduce support friction and escalation volume
- Strengthen feedback loops between delivery teams and customers

### Decision Rights
- Support-readiness recommendations for customer-facing releases
- Escalation input when customer risk is high or communications are insufficient
- Prioritization input based on support impact evidence

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Share customer context and known service pain points
- **Planning:** Define supportability requirements and communication needs
- **Execution & Tracking:** Prepare support enablement materials and validate readiness
- **Release & Deployment:** Coordinate customer communication and early support monitoring
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Report adoption outcomes and support lessons learned

### Typical Communication
- Feedback and trend reviews with Product Managers and Project Managers
- Release readiness and escalation syncs with Operations/SRE and QA/Testing
- Customer messaging coordination with stakeholders and sponsor groups

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Works with **Product Managers** to prioritize customer-impact improvements
- Coordinates with **Project Managers** on readiness checkpoints and communications
- Partners with **Release/Operations/SRE** for incident and post-release response
- Supports **Stakeholders** with customer-impact reporting

## Project Coordinator or Scrum Master / Agile Facilitator

### Role Summary
Project Coordinators or Scrum Masters improve delivery flow by facilitating ceremonies, tracking follow-through, and making impediments visible without replacing accountable role ownership.

### Responsibilities
- Facilitate ceremonies and maintain clear action follow-through
- Track blockers, dependencies, and process hygiene signals
- Support transparent board/workflow usage and team coordination
- Coach teams on lightweight, consistent collaboration practices

### Goals
- Increase delivery predictability through consistent team cadence
- Reduce hidden blockers and stalled work
- Improve cross-functional collaboration and continuous improvement follow-through

### Decision Rights
- Facilitation and process cadence recommendations
- Escalation of unresolved impediments to accountable leads
- Process improvement proposals based on team retrospectives

### Lifecycle Contributions
- **Initiation:** Support kickoff clarity and role alignment
- **Planning:** Facilitate planning ceremonies and dependency visibility
- **Execution & Tracking:** Keep cadence healthy, actions tracked, and impediments surfaced
- **Release & Deployment:** Coordinate readiness checkpoints and communication cadence
- **Retrospective & Closure:** Facilitate retrospectives and track improvement actions

### Typical Communication
- Daily coordination with Developers, QA/Testing, and Delivery/Project leads
- Ceremony outputs and action tracking updates for the full team
- Process health summaries for Project Managers and Stakeholders

### Handoffs and Interactions
- Supports **Project Managers** in schedule/risk coordination execution
- Works with **Delivery Leads/Engineering Managers** to surface impediments quickly
- Helps **Developers**, **QA/Testing**, and **Product Managers** keep planning and execution aligned
- Keeps **Stakeholders** informed through clear action and status visibility

## Role combinations on smaller teams

On smaller teams, one person may cover multiple personas. Combine roles intentionally and keep decision rights explicit so accountability does not become ambiguous.

- **Product Manager + Project Manager:** common when delivery complexity is moderate; define clear checkpoints for scope decisions versus schedule/risk control.
- **Delivery Lead + Technical Lead:** common in engineering-heavy teams; maintain separate moments for people/capacity decisions and architecture decisions.
- **Project Manager + Scrum Master/Coordinator:** common in lean delivery teams; preserve transparent facilitation and avoid masking unresolved escalations.
- **Support/Customer Success + QA/Testing:** possible in customer-centric teams; ensure customer feedback and release validation are both documented.
- **Security/Compliance + Technical Lead (temporary):** only when specialist capacity is limited; identify external review points for high-risk changes.

When combining roles, document:
- Which decision rights remain distinct
- Which approvals require a second reviewer or stakeholder sign-off
- Which handoffs are replaced by explicit checklist checkpoints
